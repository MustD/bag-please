### The finding underneath the zero

The number alone is misleading, and the correction is the most important thing in this retrospective.

Epic 5 produced **two** kinds of commitment. They performed completely differently:

| Kind                                             | Epic 6 result                                                      |
|--------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Agreements encoded into an artifact**          | ✅ Held                                                            |
| **Agreements left as prose in a retro document** | ❌ Broken, including one written *because* it had just been broken |
| **Action items as rows in a retro table**        | ❌ 0 / 7                                                           |

The clearest instance: Epic 5's lesson *"cross-workflow debt must live in `deferred-work.md`, never only in a story's
prose"* was **encoded as Story 6.1's AC15** — filing BUG-E6-1 and BUG-E6-2 became a gate on the story being `done`. It
executed perfectly: three bugs filed with cause, user-visible impact and a proposed server-side fix, with BUG-E6-2
explicitly marked a prerequisite for undeferring FR42/FR43. The AC even cites the orphaned FR9 item **by name** as its
own justification.

Meanwhile, Epic 5's agreement *"`sprint-status.yaml` is reconciled at story close regardless of which dev workflow ran"*
— written specifically because Epic 5's tracking drifted — produced an Epic 6 with **no `epic-6` block at all**. Worse
than Epic 5, which at least had a key sitting at the wrong status.

This is Epic 5's own key insight #1 (*"a gate wired into config outlives a gate written into an agreement"*) replaying
one level up: the retrospective wrote seven agreements and then behaved exactly as its own insight predicted.

**Consequence, adopted:** Epic 6's action items are recorded as **either done-in-retro or a named Epic 7 story slot**.
Nothing is left as an aspiration owned by a table.

## What Went Well

### The backend freeze held a second consecutive epic — cleanly

Zero files under `bp_back/` in either story, git-verified per story against its own baseline. The one `bp_back/` change
in the window (`8fa0162`, touching `CLAUDE.md` and the `Dockerfile`) landed **before** Epic 6 started, so the epic-level
claim survives inspection too. Every story carried a **Block If** escalation path rather than a workaround.

### Naming the central hazard in planning is what made the epic safe

AR-E6-1 stated up front that `saveItem` is a **full-document upsert keyed by id, not a partial patch** — any field
absent from `ItemInput` is written back as its default. That single ruling produced, in order:

1. The edit payload carrying `checked` and `recurring` forward.
2. The discovery that `recurring` was selected by **no** frontend document at all, so `ItemsQuery` had to be widened
   before an edit could preserve it.
3. Three genuine server-side defects **found rather than shipped silently** (BUG-E6-1/2/3).

Had the story treated `saveItem` as a patch — the natural reading — editing an item mid-shop would have silently
unchecked it.

### Story 6.1 proved its regression tests were not vacuous

Deviation 3: the carry-forward was **deliberately broken** (`checked: false, recurring: null`), the production image
rebuilt, and both regression tests confirmed to **fail on `chromium` and `mobile`**, before the code was restored.
"Silently wipes a field" is precisely the defect a green test lies about most easily. This is hand-rolled mutation
testing on the epic's highest-risk defect class, and it is the single best practice the epic produced.

### BUG-E6-2 was observed, not inferred

During the mandated manual browser pass, an item checked off through the UI came back `checked: true, checkedAt: null`
after a UI rename — confirming the `checkedAt` reset **live** before it was written up. The
"manually-exercise-first" rule earned its keep for the fourth consecutive epic.

### The review pass caught the epic's own defect class reintroduced one level up

Story 6.1's carry-forward fields were initially read from the open-time `shown` snapshot, so a co-member's check-off —
or the hourly recurring scheduler — landing while the dialog sat open would be **silently reverted by the save**.
Exactly the defect the story existed to prevent, one abstraction higher. The fix reads from the live `item` prop while
deliberately keeping the *change comparison* on the snapshot, because "did the user change anything" is a question about
what they were shown. That distinction is subtle and correct.

### A tool declined to run, and its advice was taken

`bmad-dev-auto` halted on a dirty working tree, correctly reasoned that the entire Epic 6 specification existed only as
an uncommitted `epics.md` change, **deliberately skipped** compiling epic context rather than caching an artifact
derived from uncommitted input, and recommended re-invoking **per story, smallest first**. That recommendation was
followed exactly: 6.2 (`5d56e58`) before 6.1 (`62dec3b`). A halt being respected is worth as much as the code.

### It shipped, and it shipped fast

md's assessment: fast to implement, and the production deploy went well given the volume of change behind it. Two
stories from a standing start to a live, smoke-tested deployment — carrying the whole Epic 5 reframe with them, since
that had never been deployed either.

## What Didn't Go Well

### The dominant pattern: assertions that could not fail

**6 of the 17 review patches** were the same defect class — a test that passes whether or not the thing it guards is
broken. Not one of them was a *wrong* assertion; each was an assertion incapable of detecting its own failure mode.

| Story | Assertion                                         | Why it could not fail                                                                                                |
|-------|---------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 6.2   | `barBox.height <= 72` guarding a two-line app bar | Two-line h6 is ~64px — a 56px Toolbar absorbs it entirely                                                            |
| 6.2   | Unauthenticated case via `browser.newContext()`   | Doesn't inherit the project `use` block → ran **desktop on the `mobile` project**, voiding the mandatory mobile gate |
| 6.2   | `.focus()` to exercise the focus ring             | `.focus()` does not produce `:focus-visible` in Chromium                                                             |
| 6.2   | Admin `toHaveURL(/\/lists$/)` right after `goto`  | Satisfiable by a transient state; a future admin bounce would still pass                                             |
| 6.2   | The `flexGrow: 1`-on-the-wrapper invariant        | Entirely untested — the one design decision the change exists to protect                                             |
| 6.1   | "No suggestions on a store-less list"             | Fired while `cache-and-network` was in flight — passed either way                                                    |
| 6.1   | `edit-item-error`                                 | Only ever asserted **absent**; the `catch` branch every Epic 5 convention protects never once executed               |

The antidote already existed in the same epic, in the same story, applied to 2 of 20 tests (deviation 3 above). The
manual-browser-pass rule proved the *feature* worked; nothing proved the *tests* worked.

### The tracking agreement written last epic was broken this epic

`sprint-status.yaml` had **no Epic 6 entries whatsoever** — not the epic, not the stories, not the retrospective key the
retro workflow targets. Both Epic 6 specs inherit Epic 5's stance that sprint tracking belongs to a separate flow.
Corrected in this pass, but the mechanism that produced it is untouched: no dev workflow owns reconciliation.

### The image publish path was broken for two epics and no gate could see it

`images-build-push.sh` built the frontend image with `./bp_front` as context, while `bp_front/Dockerfile` requires the
**repo root** (`COPY routing/Caddyfile /etc/caddy/Caddyfile`). The script has not been touched since `9cfbd64`, which
predates Epic 1; Epic 5's Caddy rewrite broke it and it stayed broken through Epics 5 and 6, surfacing only when md
deployed by hand. Fixed in `fe31fbf`.

The structural lesson outlives the one-line fix. Epic 5's flagship insight was *"test the shipped artifact, not the
convenient one"* — implemented as `webServer: docker compose up -d --build`. But compose builds with `context: .` and
the publish script built separately: **two different build paths, and the one that actually ships to the registry was
never in any gate.** The principle was right; the coverage stopped exactly where the artifact leaves the machine. And
the fix that made the deployment reproducible sat **uncommitted in the working tree** until this retro.

### FR47 ran in production without its dry-run

Action item #5 asked for validation against a snapshot, twice, before deploying. The migration's first and only real
execution was against production data. It succeeded — and the design deserves credit: genuinely idempotent via the
`app_migrations` `epic4-list-seed` record, no-ops when no unscoped data exists, and fails loudly rather than silently on
a missing `MIGRATION_TARGET_USER`. But succeeded-unvalidated is not validated; the risk simply did not materialise, in
the least recoverable place available.

### Uninspected signals, now compounding

- Both Epic 6 specs carry `warnings: [oversized]`; Story 6.1 added **`multiple-goals`**. Third epic ignored — and
  `bmad-dev-auto` had independently flagged the multiple-goals risk *in its own blocked report* before the epic ran.
- `followup_review_recommended: true` on **both** specs; neither follow-up ran during the epic. 6.1's was raised because
  the review pass *changed data-write semantics* (the carry-forward source) — not a cosmetic set. Discharged at this
  retro.
- `bmad-dev-auto-result-epic-6.md` sat at `status: blocked` for the whole epic. Read cold, it says Epic 6 never started.
- Both stories ran on `epic-4-lists`, a branch name two epics stale. Flagged twice, no consequence, never acted on.

### The `registrationEnabled` flake, accepted for the 6th and 7th time

Epic 5's own agreement — *"a flake may be accepted once with a recorded reason; the second re-acceptance creates a
scheduled fix"* — was broken twice more, and Epic 6 added **+34 UI registrations per full run**, measurably widening the
window. The `toPass()` workaround is now copy-pasted into a **fourth** spec file, which is itself why the fix cannot be
made in one place. One post-story run showed 4 flaky (all in the untouched `lists.spec.ts`, all `"Registration is
disabled"`, all retry-healed).

### FR57 was shipped without ever entering the PRD

FR57 was authored during Epic 6 epic-planning and lives only in `epics.md`. `prd.md` tops out at FR56. A shipped,
E2E-covered, production-deployed requirement with no entry in the requirements document. **Fixed in this retro.**

## Cross-Story Technical Patterns

### The `saveItem` defect family — one server-side change closes all three

| Bug          | Cause                                                                                                               | User-visible impact                                                                                                                                                      |
|--------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **BUG-E6-1** | `addedBy` is server-set from the caller's principal and is not in `ItemInput` (`ItemApi.kt:52`)                     | A co-member fixing a typo **steals authorship**; the shopping view's `addedBy` avatar flips silently                                                                     |
| **BUG-E6-2** | `mapItemFromInput` builds a **fresh** `Item`; `checkedAt` isn't in `ItemInput`, and `save` `set`s everything        | An edit clears the check-off clock, so a checked recurring item **never comes back** — the scheduler `continue`s on `checkedAt == null`. **Prerequisite for FR42/FR43.** |
| **BUG-E6-3** | `ItemStorage.delete` is a hard delete while `ItemRepository.save` blind-upserts, and `/lists/:id` is refetch-driven | A stale open dialog can **resurrect a deleted item** for everyone, or write a dangling `category` making an item invisible-but-present — recovery needs DB access        |

All three reduce to: **`saveItem` constructs a new document instead of merging onto the stored one.** The fix is a
single change in `ItemService.saveItem` — load the existing `Item`, `copy()` only the fields `ItemInput` carries, and
reject an upsert whose id does not exist. It requires the first deliberate backend unfreeze in three epics.

All three **ship in production today** by explicit epic decision.

### Conventions that held without being re-litigated

Every Epic 5 form/feedback convention was applied verbatim and none was re-discovered: validate on submit only, the
same-tick `if (loading) return` re-entry guard, a real `catch` on every async branch, inline errors via
`<Alert role="alert">` never toasts, no success toast, `helperText ?? ' '` to reserve layout space, and
`onClose()` → `void onDone().catch(() => {})` so a failed refresh is never reported as a failed save. The severity curve
supports it: **0 intent gaps and 0 spec defects across both stories**, so no repair loopback ran on either.

### Two deliberate, well-argued deviations

1. **Chips, not `Autocomplete`, for store suggestions.** Three existing specs select the category `Select` as
   `getByTestId('add-item-dialog').getByRole('combobox')`; a second combobox is a strict-mode violation that would break
   `lists.spec.ts:109`, `lists.spec.ts:162` and `shopping.spec.ts:79`. Clickable `Chip`s also match the UX spec and make
   the empty case trivially absent.
2. **`normalizeStore` lives in `src/lib/lists/storeValue.ts`, not in `StoreField.tsx`.**
   `react-refresh/only-export-components` rejects a component file that also exports a plain function, and zero warnings
   is an AC. Still **one** normalizer imported by all three consumers, so the invariant the spec cared about survives.

Both were recorded as deviations with reasoning rather than quietly absorbed. That is the correct handling.

## Key Insights

1. **A commitment that isn't encoded in an artifact isn't a commitment.** Epic 5's ledger rule became an acceptance
   criterion and executed perfectly. Epic 5's seven action-item rows went 0/7, and its brand-new tracking agreement was
   broken in the very next epic. The mechanism, not the discipline, is the variable.

2. **A test is unproven until it has been seen failing.** Six of seventeen patches were assertions incapable of
   detecting what they were written for. The epic contained its own antidote — break it, watch it go red on both
   projects, restore — and applied it to 2 of 20 tests.

3. **"Test the shipped artifact" has to include the path that ships it.** The E2E gate built with one Docker context and
   the publish script with another. Nothing exercised the second, so the frontend image was unbuildable for two epics
   and only a hand deployment revealed it.

4. **Naming the highest-risk defect class in planning is worth more than any amount of care while coding.** AR-E6-1 (
   "full-document upsert, not a patch") is the reason three latent server-side bugs were found and filed rather than
   shipped invisibly.

5. **Deploying discharges debt that no amount of scheduling did.** Two items open since Epic 4 — real-device validation
   and FR47 migration — were resolved by the act of shipping, after being carried across three retrospectives. Some debt
   is only ever paid by the real environment.

6. **A halt is a deliverable.** `bmad-dev-auto` refusing to run on an uncommitted specification, and explaining why
   caching a derived artifact would compound the problem, prevented an implementation diff entangled with its own
   unreviewed source of truth.

## Team Agreements (updated)

Carried forward unchanged:

- **E2E tests are UI tests.** API calls permitted only for environment preparation.
- **Every E2E test names its FR (s)** in the title.
- **A story is not `done` until E2E coverage exists for its critical user-facing path**, on desktop **and** mobile,
  against the production image.
- **Manually exercise each flow in a real browser before writing its E2E.** (Paid for itself again — BUG-E6-2.)
- **Deferred/tracked debt goes in `deferred-work.md`**, not only in a story file or spec. *(The one Epic 5 agreement
  that worked — because Epic 6 encoded it as an AC.)*
- **Previous retro action items reviewed at the start of every retro before new items are added.**
- **If backend changes are needed, that is an explicit `md` decision and an explicit unfreeze**, never an inline
  workaround.

New from Epic 6:

- **A new test is unproven until it has been observed failing.** Break the behaviour it guards, confirm red on **both**
  projects, restore. Recorded in `project-context.md`.
- **`browser.newContext()` does not inherit the project `use` block.** Prefer the `page` fixture; when a second actor
  needs its own context, put the actor whose *rendering* the mobile gate must cover on `page`.
- **A retro action item must become a story or an acceptance criterion.** A row in a table is not a commitment. Epic 6's
  items are recorded as done-in-retro or as named next-epic story slots — nothing else.
- **A new functional requirement authored during epic planning is added to `prd.md` in that same planning pass.** FR57
  shipped to production without ever appearing there.
- **Never infer production configuration from this repo.** Production config is server-only by deliberate security
  decision; `docker-compose.yaml` is the dev/E2E topology and nothing more.
